Retriangulation Redrawn

Bold lines are the rays the Ordnance Survey observed between its trig stations in 1936–62. The faint web is every pair of standing pillars with a clear line of sight over the terrain.

* in the 2010–15 Copernicus surface model

    Models, not observations. Lines are tested over bare ground with standard refraction, then against a radar surface model from 2010–15 for trees and buildings. Hedgerows and recent growth are missed.

    How this was made

    Pillars are the 6,079 standing pillars in the OS Complete Trig Archive. Sight height is the archive height of the flush bracket, raised to the pillar top (1.22 m above ground) plus 0.25 m for an instrument.

    Each pair up to 250 km apart was tested against OS Terrain 50, a 50 m bare-earth model. The test allows for Earth curvature and a refraction coefficient of 0.13, ignoring 100 m at each end. 230,499 pairs are clear. Each clear pair was then re-tested on the Copernicus GLO-30 surface model, which includes trees and buildings as radar saw them in 2010–15: 200,670 are still clear.

    Checked against the 641 pillar-to-pillar rays the surveyors actually observed between 1936 and 1962, the model finds 95.5% of them.
